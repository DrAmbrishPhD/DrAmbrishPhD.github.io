    01 / ABOUT

    A career built across the evidence ecosystem.

    I am an epidemiologist and health economist with more than a decade of experience spanning evidence generation, evidence synthesis, real-world evidence, health economics and health technology assessment.

    My work deliberately crosses the boundary between primary and secondary research. I have worked on randomised trials, longitudinal epidemiology, observational and healthcare database studies, burden-of-disease research, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, network meta-analysis, economic evaluation and HTA/value dossiers.

    My PhD at the Menzies Institute for Medical Research, University of Tasmania, examined the epidemiology, correlates and economic implications of early knee osteoarthritis. That training shaped a way of working that remains central to my practice: understand the population, interrogate the data, synthesise what is known and identify what decision-makers still need to know.

    I currently lead evidence generation and synthesis for a global cardiometabolic health portfolio, directing a team of HTA scientists and serving as scientific lead on high-complexity, regulator-facing real-world evidence deliverables developed with academic and clinical collaborators worldwide.
